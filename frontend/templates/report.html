7. Method of examination

The following independent tests were applied to the item.

File identity Magic-byte signature detection, comparison of the claimed extension against the real container, and a search for embedded secondary file structures.
Metadata Extraction of EXIF, XMP and IPTC data, followed by internal cross-checking of capture time against modification time, camera serial against make and model, and recorded dimensions against stored pixels.
Error level analysis Re-compression at a fixed quality factor and computation of the residual, measured block by block across the JPEG grid.
Copy-move detection Perceptual DCT hashing of every block, with matching against neighbouring blocks to locate duplicated regions.
Noise and splicing High-pass residual analysis of the frame, with measurement of noise discontinuity across block boundaries.
Resampling Spectral analysis of the image to detect the periodic signature left by interpolation, and the loss of high-frequency energy caused by enlargement.
Steganography Chi-square analysis of the least significant bit plane of each colour channel, and a check for data appended beyond the end of the image stream.
Content Classification of the scene content, cross-checked against the recorded metadata for contradiction.
Qualification and limitations

This report records the output of an automated analytical process. Each finding is an indicator that warrants investigation; none of them is proof of manipulation on its own. Conversely, the absence of a finding does not establish that an item is authentic. The results depend on the item as received: an image that has been re-encoded, re-saved or transmitted through a messaging platform will have lost some of the characteristics that these tests look for, and that loss should not be read as evidence of innocence.

The examination was carried out on a standalone workstation. The item was fingerprinted with SHA-256 and SHA-512 at receipt and a sealed copy was retained. No data was transmitted off the machine.
